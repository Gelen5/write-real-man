# Workflow: Choose Topic

Use this for a topic the user supplies, a live-trend candidate, or an evergreen idea. Read `references/topic-hook-and-resonance.md` as part of topic approval.

1. **Find the lived moment.** Name one reader, what they are doing, what they already have in hand, the exact snag, and what becomes easier if it is solved. Avoid broad labels such as “everyone wants to use AI.”
2. **Choose one useful claim.** State what the article will help the reader see or do. Identify the obvious first impulse and the more useful angle the article can support. This is a possible reader assumption to examine, not a claim that everyone thinks alike.
3. **Check the tension against evidence.** The surprising turn must come from an attributable source, a verified product constraint, or a concrete step-by-step example. Separate observed fact from editorial inference. If the reversal is only dramatic wording, soften it or reject the idea.
4. **Write the payoff before the headline.** Specify what the reader can decide, try, copy, check or avoid by the end. A title may hold back the explanation, but the article must pay off its promise.
5. **Pitch a hook, then audit it.** Draft a clear title using a grounded contrast, a familiar mistake with a real consequence, or a concrete unresolved question. The title should make sense on its own, identify a recognizable situation, and leave one useful question open. Avoid explaining the entire article in the title.
6. **Run the five-part editorial gate.** Check reader recognition, concrete stakes, evidence-backed cognitive gap, open curiosity, and deliverable payoff. Keep an idea only when the gap is evidence-backed, the payoff passes, and at least four of the five checks pass. This is an editorial readiness check, not a click-through prediction.
7. **Separate the labels.** Mark `trend`, `hybrid` or `evergreen`; preserve trend score, hook judgment and evidence quality as different things. Do not call a high trend score “high click potential.”
8. **Choose the form.** Select `scenario-tutorial`, `beginner-guide`, `use-case`, `problem-solution`, `ordinary-person-news` or `practical-opinion`. Read `references/audience-ordinary-people.md` and `references/scenario-first.md` before drafting.

If the topic is useful but flat, change the angle before polishing the headline. If no truthful tension survives, choose a different topic instead of manufacturing a contrarian claim.
