# Topic Hook and Resonance Gate

Use this before presenting a topic shortlist, selecting an idea to write, or finalizing a title. This reference adapts four editorial lenses: `dbs-content` (single core claim and cognitive gap), `dbs-resonate` (a real audience situation and supported emotional path), `dbs-hook` (topic + hook + credibility), and `dbs-spread` (why someone might keep, discuss or share the piece). These are writing checks, not a prediction model.

## Start with the reader's moment

Describe one ordinary situation in one sentence: **who is doing what, what is in front of them, and where they get stuck**. Name the practical or social consequence without inflating it. “A person with groceries doesn't know what to cook” is recognizable but still broad; “there are three half-used ingredients, one expires soon, and dinner needs to be ready in 20 minutes” gives the article a scene to work with. This is an illustrative scenario, not a claim about actual users.

Then define:

- **One core claim:** what the reader should understand or do differently.
- **One useful gap:** what the obvious first answer misses, or which detail the reader still cannot judge.
- **One payoff:** a specific action, decision, checklist or checked draft the article will deliver.

If a reader could reasonably respond “I know this already,” find a less obvious cause, constraint, cost or next step. Do not add a twist just to sound clever.

## Build a truthful hook

Use a possible assumption as a starting point, not as a proven belief held by the public. Find an observable mismatch:

`recognizable situation + expected move + evidence-backed complication + useful payoff`

The complication can be a missed condition, a hidden trade-off, a mistake that changes the result, or a smaller action that works better for this specific case. It must be supportable by the collected signal, official documentation, the supplied material, or a clearly labeled exercise. Do not turn an inference into a general fact.

Draft one strongest title and, when useful, two alternatives using different doors:

- **Grounded reversal:** “周报别急着让 AI 写漂亮：先把没完成和没等回复的事列出来”
- **Concrete consequence:** name what can be missed, delayed, misread or wasted, only when the article demonstrates it.
- **Unresolved practical question:** ask which detail changes the decision; answer it in the article.

Prefer a title that leaves the explanation open but makes the promised benefit legible. Avoid generic openings such as “AI 正在改变生活,” “AI 赋能,” “X 个技巧助你……,” or a plain feature description. Avoid fake urgency, inflated danger, unsupported “大家都在” claims, and artificial “原来真正的原因是” reversals.

## Five checks before a topic is accepted

Mark each `pass`, `revise` or `fail` and cite the evidence or planned reader action:

1. **Recognition:** Can the intended reader identify a real moment or object from the title and opening?
2. **Stakes:** Is there a concrete inconvenience, decision, risk or desire that matters to that reader, beyond “AI is interesting”?
3. **Cognitive gap:** Does the angle reveal a useful complication or reframe? Is it supported, rather than merely phrased as the opposite of a familiar claim?
4. **Curiosity:** Does the title leave one worthwhile question open without hiding what kind of help the article offers?
5. **Payoff:** Can the planned article answer the title and end with something the reader can do, check, save or decide?

Accept only if the cognitive gap is evidence-backed, the payoff passes, and at least four of the five checks pass. If checks 1, 2 or 4 need work, sharpen the scene, stakes or title and review again. If evidence or payoff fails, reject the topic or change its promise. Do not display a numeric “click score.”

## Diagnose resonance without pretending to know the audience

- Keep one main claim; subordinate product facts and tips to it.
- Tie any emotional reading to the actual situation and wording. Say “可能会让人犹豫” when that is an inference; do not write “用户都很焦虑” without evidence.
- Keep usefulness, recognition, surprise, commentability and shareability distinct. A saveable checklist is not automatically a shareable opinion.
- Do not predict clicks, completion, virality or platform distribution. The gate assesses whether the editorial promise is clear and worth testing.

## Example: reject the flat angle, keep the useful task

| Draft | Editorial decision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “让 AI 根据冰箱食材生成一周菜单” | Revise | It names an action, but no particular moment, tension or reason to open is visible. |
| “下班后冰箱里菜不少，为什么还是不知道吃什么？让 AI 排菜单前，先说清你有几分钟” | Candidate to test | The scene is recognizable and the useful complication is concrete. Treat the scenario as an exercise unless sourced; show the input and check whether the suggested meal fits the actual ingredients and time. Do not claim it is a current trend without dated evidence. |
| “AI 写周报越流畅，领导越不信任你” | Reject unless independently evidenced | It asserts a broad causal effect and invents an audience reaction. A sharper-sounding headline cannot replace proof. |

The title is an editorial hypothesis, not a guaranteed click result. Keep the article readable and deliver exactly what the headline promises.
