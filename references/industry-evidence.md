# Industry evidence and claim states

| Role | Good sources | What it can establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Event anchor | government notice, regulator, official incident report, original release | what happened, when, where, action and stated result |
| Technical capability | official docs, original paper, standard, reproducible benchmark | how a component works and documented limits |
| Deployment | procurement record, project report, named operator or customer case | where a system was used and its scope |
| Scale | official statistics or a report with methodology | a bounded count, coverage, budget, or market measure |
| Reaction | attributed practitioner or platform post | what that person reported or worried about |

Media can corroborate or add interviews. It should not replace an available primary source for the central fact.

## Claim states

- `verified_fact`: directly supported by a cited source.
- `reported_claim`: attributed to a named organization or person but not independently confirmed.
- `author_inference`: a conclusion drawn from facts; show the reasoning.
- `proposal`: a future connection, product, policy, or industry-chain design.
- `unknown`: information public evidence does not establish.

Do not silently promote a claim during drafting.

## Minimum standard

- Central event: one primary or authoritative source.
- Material number: source, date, unit, geography, and period when applicable.
- Deployment: named place or organization plus scope.
- Opportunity: the missing handoff or recurring cost that creates it.
- Causality: direct evidence or explicit inference with competing explanations.

Record live-source access dates. If current evidence is unavailable, say so rather than using model memory.
