# Humanization rules

Human writing is not random writing.

## Reduce model-like patterns

Watch for:
- identical paragraph lengths
- repeated “首先 / 其次 / 最后” scaffolding
- repeated “不仅…更…” and “这意味着…”
- every paragraph ending in a mini-summary
- excessive balanced three-part lists
- abstract nouns without examples
- conclusions that repeat earlier sentences with synonyms
- fake certainty
- generic emotional filler

## Better revision moves

Use only when they improve meaning:
- replace abstraction with an observed behavior
- turn a claim into a comparison
- add one real limitation
- cut one redundant sentence
- combine two short formulaic paragraphs
- split a dense paragraph at a conceptual turn
- move the strongest evidence earlier
- preserve a natural aside when it reflects the author’s actual voice

## Content movement before surface tone

When a paragraph still feels generated after word-level editing, inspect what
the paragraph is doing. Stronger human-readable passages often move through a
specific object, action, discrepancy, correction or decision. Weaker passages
often pause to explain the lesson, summarize the example or cover every possible
case.

Useful moves:
- continue from the example into the next observable action
- show a plausible wrong output and correct its factual error
- retain incomplete states such as “waiting for a reply” when they are true
- end once the reader has a concrete next action

If paragraph-level detector evidence is available, read
`validated-human-style.md` before revising.

## Do not

- add deliberate spelling mistakes
- inject random slang
- create fake personal anecdotes
- use arbitrary sentence-length noise
- degrade technical precision
- remove citations simply because they look formal
