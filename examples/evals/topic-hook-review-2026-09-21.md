# 0.3.1 Topic Hook Review

This is a qualitative editorial check of the new selection gate. It does not measure real audience behavior, test a platform, or predict clicks.

## Case: turn a flat everyday AI idea into a testable angle

**Raw topic:** Use AI to make a meal plan from ingredients in the fridge.

**Flat title:** “用 AI 根据冰箱食材生成一周菜单。”

| Check | Flat title | Revised candidate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition | Broad household task | Ingredients are available, but the suggested meal may not fit tonight's time |
| Stakes | Convenience in general | The reader still has to decide whether they can actually cook the suggestion |
| Cognitive gap | No distinct insight | Ingredient list alone may be insufficient for a usable dinner plan; the time limit is another input to test |
| Curiosity | The title gives the whole task | “Why doesn't the menu fit?” remains open, while the article promises a practical prompt adjustment |
| Payoff | Generic weekly menu | A short input template using real ingredients and the time available, followed by a check against the fridge |

**Revised title candidate:** “下班后冰箱里菜不少，为什么还是不知道吃什么？让 AI 排菜单前，先说清你有几分钟。”

**Decision:** Pass as an editorial candidate, not as a proven high-click title. The after-work scene and time limit are an illustrative exercise, not a research finding. The article must label the example, avoid claiming that this happens to most people, and show the reader how to check whether the proposed meal uses the ingredients and fits the time limit. If the demo does not support the promised difference, revise or reject the title.

## Negative control: dramatic claim without support

**Title:** “AI 写周报越流畅，领导越不信任你。”

**Decision:** Reject. It asserts a broad causal relationship and invents a manager's reaction. Replace it with a demonstrable task-level tension, such as a polished report accidentally obscuring which work is still waiting for a reply, and only if the article actually shows that distinction.

## Acceptance note

The new gate distinguishes four things that were previously collapsed: reader recognition, practical value, evidence-backed novelty, and title curiosity. Passing it means an editor can explain the promise and support it with material; it does not mean a reader will click.
